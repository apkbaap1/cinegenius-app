01 · What is OpenFilm

A video editor that Claude can drive.

OpenFilm is a skill for Claude. Once it's installed, you describe the video you want and Claude builds it — every shot, the music and the cuts — and opens it in an editor where you can watch and change everything.

How it works

A film is one web page, film.html. Each shot is its own small page drawn in code, and footage, pictures and sound are clips placed on a timeline.

Because it's all plain files, Claude can read and change any part of it precisely.

What you can make

  • Launch and promo videos
  • Explainers and tutorials
  • Reels and Shorts (9:16)
  • Motion graphics and title cards
  • Edits of your own footage

03 · Install

One command. That's it.

Open a terminal in the folder of your project (or any folder you like) and run:

$ npx skills add openfilm/openfilm
◇ Cloning repository…
✓ Installed 1 skill · openfilm

This adds the skill to the project (.agents/skills/openfilm) and links it for Claude Code (.claude/skills/openfilm). Start, or restart, Claude Code in that folder and the skill is available.

Tip: commit these files to your repository so the skill comes with the project on every machine and every session.
04 · Your first video

Just ask Claude

Describe what you want in plain words — any language works. Be specific about length, shape and style:

Make a 20-second launch video for my app, 16:9, dark and cinematic.
Make a vertical 9:16 Reel explaining what an AI agent is, with captions.

05 · OpenFilm Studio

Watch every change land, live.

Studio is OpenFilm's editor. It opens in your browser with a preview on top and a timeline below, and it updates the moment Claude changes the film.

Opening Studio

Claude opens it for you when it starts a film. To open it yourself:

$ npx -y openfilm@latest open my-film # a new film gets its own folder
$ npx -y openfilm@latest open # reopen the film you're in, or the last one

It prints Studio's address; open it in your browser and keep it open while you work with Claude.

What's in a project

my-film/
├── film.html     ← the film: tracks of clips on a timeline
├── title.html    ← one shot, drawn in code
├── shot-2.html   ← another shot
├── assets/       ← video, pictures and sound
└── .film/        ← Studio's own settings — leave it alone

Reading the timeline

Tracks

Each row is a track. Tracks higher up are drawn on top — titles above footage, background at the bottom.

Clips

A clip is a shot page, a video, a picture or a sound. Clips on one track never overlap.

Locked tracks

Lock a track in Studio and Claude won't touch anything on it. Use this to protect your own edits.

Hidden & muted

Hide a track to stop it showing, or mute it to silence it, without deleting anything.

06 · Editing, two ways

Drag it, or just say it.

① In Studio

  • Drag a clip to move it in time
  • Drag a clip's edges to trim it
  • Move clips between tracks
  • Edit text and elements on the picture

② Tell Claude

  • Describe the change in plain words
  • Claude changes only what you asked
  • Your Studio edits are kept
  • Watch it update live in Studio

Things you can ask for

Make the title stay on screen longer.
Swap shots 2 and 3.
Speed up the intro.
Cut the part where I repeat myself in clip 4.
Make the captions bigger and move the logo to the top right.

What changes under the hood

Every request becomes a small, precise change in film.html. Knowing these helps you ask for exactly what you want:

SettingWhat it controlsExample
TrimWhich seconds of a clip playclip.mp4#t=2,8 plays 2 s to 8 s
atWhere a clip starts in the filmat="12.5"
speedFaster or slower, 0.25× to 4×speed="1.5"
volumeA clip's loudness (1 = original)volume="0.3" for music under voice
PositionWhere a clip sits and its sizeleft, top, width, rotate
No quality loss: trimming never cuts or re-encodes your footage. Every clip is the same original file with its own in and out points.

07 · Voice, music & captions

Add sound with your own keys.

AI voice-over, music, sound effects, transcripts and images come from services you connect with your own API key.

1

Open Settings

In Studio, click the OpenFilm logo at the top left.
2

Connect a provider

Under Providers, add your key for a service.
3

Pick models

Under Services, choose which model makes each kind of media.
You wantServices that make it
Voice-overElevenLabs, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft Azure, Groq, Xiaomi MiMo
Music & sound effectsElevenLabs, fal.ai
Transcripts / captionsElevenLabs, OpenAI, Google, Groq, Doubao, Xiaomi MiMo
Images & videoOpenAI, Google, fal.ai · image search: Pexels, Pixabay
Already have a voice-over? Just give Claude the file. It places it on the timeline and times the captions to your words. Captions are styled in Studio — size, colour, position and a word-by-word highlight.
08 · Export & share

Get your MP4

From Studio

Use Export to save the finished video, with subtitles burned in if you want them.

Or ask Claude

"Render the video" — it saves an MP4 next to film.html.
$ npx -y openfilm@latest render # 30 fps MP4 beside film.html
$ npx -y openfilm@latest render --subtitles # captions burned in

09 · Tips & fixes

Work faster, avoid surprises.

Pro tips

Say the shape first

"9:16 for Reels" or "16:9 for YouTube" before anything else — the layout depends on it.

One request at a time

Small, clear changes are easier to check and undo than one long list.

Lock what you love

Lock a track in Studio once it's perfect, so later changes can't touch it.

Name your clips

"Clip 4", "the title", "the intro" — Claude follows the names you use.

Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
Skill not foundRestart Claude Code in the folder where you ran the install command.
Studio didn't openRun npx -y openfilm@latest open and open the printed address yourself.
"Not connected" for voice/musicAdd your key in Studio → Settings → Providers.
A clip won't play in the previewAsk Claude to check it; some generated videos need a one-time re-encode for the browser.
Telugu/Hindi captions look brokenInstall a font for that script (e.g. Noto Sans Telugu) and restart Studio.

Cheat sheet

$ npx skills add openfilm/openfilm     # install the skill
$ npx -y openfilm@latest open my-film # open or create a film
$ npx -y openfilm@latest look          # contact sheet of the film
$ npx -y openfilm@latest render        # export an MP4
