# "One Ring at a Time": how Telangana became a state. Script v1 (2026-11-17)
Format: cartoon-3d-short (2D bean-head cards in a painted 3D world), vertical, about 60 s at about 3.7 words/s.
Tone: inspirational, one steady voice, English only, no captions. Story, characters and voice are our own.

## Facts (checked before the script; sources at the end)
1. 1 Nov 1956: the Telangana region of Hyderabad State joins Andhra State to form Andhra Pradesh (States Reorganisation Act 1956). The 1955 States Reorganisation Commission had advised keeping Telangana separate until at least 1961. [1][2]
2. Feb 1956: the "Gentlemen's Agreement" between leaders of the two regions gives Telangana safeguards: jobs, education seats and regional spending. [1][3]
3. 1969: the "Jai Telangana" agitation, led largely by students, says the safeguards were not kept. Hundreds die in police firing (the commonly cited figure is about 370). [1][4]
4. Prof. Kothapalli Jayashankar (1934-2011): economist and the movement's best-known thinker for decades. Died 21 June 2011. In 2014 the new state's agricultural university was renamed Professor Jayashankar Telangana State Agricultural University. [5][6]
5. 2001: K. Chandrashekar Rao founds the Telangana Rashtra Samithi. 29 Nov 2009: he begins a fast. 9 Dec 2009: the Union government announces it will start forming Telangana. 23 Dec 2009: it puts the process on hold. [1][7]
6. 10 Mar 2011: "Million March" at Tank Bund, on Hyderabad's Hussain Sagar lake. [1]
7. 13 Sep 2011: "Sakala Janula Samme" (strike of all the people), about 42 days. Government staff, transport, teachers and Singareni coal miners stop work, which causes power shortages. [1][8]
8. Feb 2014: Parliament passes the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Bill (Lok Sabha 18 Feb, Rajya Sabha 20 Feb). 2 June 2014: Telangana becomes India's 29th state. Hyderabad stays the shared capital for up to 10 years. [1][9]
9. Population about 35 million (2011 census). [10]
10. Bathukamma: Telangana's autumn flower festival. Women stack flowers (tangedu, gunugu and others) in rings into a cone-shaped tower. [11]

Sources: [1] Wikipedia, "Telangana movement". [2] Report of the States Reorganisation Commission (1955), paras 369-389. [3] Wikipedia, "Gentlemen's agreement of Andhra Pradesh". [4] Wikipedia, "1969 Telangana movement". [5] Wikipedia, "Kothapalli Jayashankar". [6] PJTSAU (pjtsau.edu.in), "About". [7] The Hindu, 10 Dec 2009 and 24 Dec 2009. [8] Wikipedia, "Sakala Janula Samme". [9] Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014 (India Code). [10] Census of India 2011. [11] Wikipedia, "Bathukamma".

## Beat formula adapted for an inspirational short
The bridge formula is deadpan comedy. Here the same skeleton carries a rise:
hook (the huge number) → setting (1956, the promise) → escalation (1969, the quiet decades, 2009, 2011) → the snap becomes the breakthrough (2014) → the explanation becomes the Bathukamma analogy → the consequence → a callback to the professor.
There is no "Luckily / Unluckily" joke line: the topic is people's sacrifice, so the voice stays warm and never jokes.

## Script (one line per beat, with visual notes in brackets)
1. Thirty-five million people waited fifty-eight years for one date on the calendar.
   [hook: a calendar page in a crowded square flips through decades, stops on blank]
2. In 1956, the Telangana region of Hyderabad was joined with Andhra to make one big Telugu state.
   [setting: golden aerial over a painted Deccan town, two halves of a map card slide together]
3. Its leaders signed a promise: Telangana's jobs, schools and money would stay protected.
   [MS: a paper being signed and folded into a pocket]
4. By 1969, students said that promise was being broken, and "Jai Telangana" filled the streets.
   [glide down a street of students, crowd mode march]
5. Hundreds lost their lives. The streets went quiet, but the idea didn't.
   [golden: empty street, one lantern stays lit in a window; FLASH_AT is not used here, a soft fade instead]
6. A professor named Jayashankar spent decades carrying it, village to village, like a lesson he refused to end.
   [day: an old teacher card walks through villages, a chalkboard, children listening]
7. In 2009, a hunger strike by K. Chandrashekar Rao made Delhi say yes. Two weeks later, Delhi said wait.
   [a far-off domed building card; a "yes" stamp, then a pause gesture]
8. So in 2011, Telangana simply stopped.
   [hard cut to silence: a bus halts, an office door shuts]
9. For forty-two days, buses, offices, schools, even the coal mines went still.
   [quick creeping inserts: idle bus, empty desk, a miner's lamp switching off]
10. A Million March was called to the lake at Tank Bund, and the crowds came singing.
   [wide over the lake, huge crowd, flower garlands]
11. In February 2014, Parliament passed the bill.
   [the breakthrough: a gavel lands, kick shake]
12. And on the second of June, Telangana became India's twenty-ninth state.
   [white flash, fireworks over the town, day turns to golden]
13. Every autumn, women here build Bathukamma, a tower of flowers, one ring at a time.
   [analogy: women stacking yellow and pink rings on a plate, close-up with lens blur]
14. No single flower makes the tower. Each ring just rests on the one below.
   [the cone rises ring by ring]
15. That's how this state was built: every generation added one ring.
   [each ring dissolves into the crowd of its era: 1969 students, the professor, 2011 marchers]
16. Professor Jayashankar died in 2011, three years too early to see it.
   [the teacher's chalkboard, an empty chair]
17. So the new state named its farm university after him, for the man who planted the idea.
   [callback: a seed in a field, then a wide of green fields at golden hour; the last flower goes on top]

Word count: 227 (about 61 s at 3.7 words/s). Target 205-250 words for 55-68 s.

## Visual rules for this topic
- No party flags or party colours (pink, green-white) anywhere. Use the national tricolour, Bathukamma flowers and the Charminar silhouette as the place marker.
- No real person's face drawn as a likeness. The professor and the strike leader are generic cards (glasses and shawl for the professor).
- No violence on screen for 1969: the loss is told through the empty street and the lantern.
- Do not show self-harm or suicide in any form.
- Andhra is never shown as a villain. The story is about a promise and persistence, not about blame.
