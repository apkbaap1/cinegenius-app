# "One Ring at a Time": how Telangana became a state. Script v2 (2026-11-17, fact-checked)
Format: cartoon-3d-short (2D bean-head cards in a painted 3D world), vertical, about 60 s at about 3.7 words/s.
Tone: inspirational, one steady voice, no captions. Voice-over in English (main) and Telugu (second version), both recorded by the user. Story, characters and voice are our own.

## Facts (checked before the script; sources at the end)
1. 1 Nov 1956: the Telangana region of Hyderabad State joins Andhra State to form Andhra Pradesh (States Reorganisation Act 1956). The 1955 States Reorganisation Commission had advised keeping Telangana separate until at least 1961. [1][2]
2. 20 Feb 1956, Hyderabad House, Delhi: the "Gentlemen's Agreement", signed by 4 leaders from each region, gives Telangana safeguards against discrimination in jobs, education and spending. Later breaches of it are a main cause of the movement. [3][12]
3. 1969: the "Jai Telangana" agitation, led largely by students, says the safeguards were not kept. Hundreds die, most in police firing. The figure on the Gun Park memorial is 369; other accounts say about 300. There is no official count, so the script says only "hundreds". [4]
4. Prof. Kothapalli Jayashankar (1934-2011): economist and the movement's best-known ideologue, active from 1952 as a student leader until his death. Died 21 June 2011. In July 2014 the new state named its agricultural university (split from ANGRAU) Professor Jayashankar Telangana State Agricultural University. [5][6]
5. 2001: K. Chandrashekar Rao founds the Telangana Rashtra Samithi. 29 Nov 2009: he begins a fast. 9 Dec 2009: the Union government announces it will start forming Telangana. 23 Dec 2009: it puts the process on hold. [1][7]
6. 10 Mar 2011: "Million March" at Tank Bund, on Hyderabad's Hussain Sagar lake. About 50,000 people reached the venue, so the script says "tens of thousands", not a million. [1]
7. 13 Sep 2011: "Sakala Janula Samme" (strike of all the people), about 42 days. Ended 24 Oct 2011. Government employees, lawyers (courts boycotted), about 60,000 Singareni coal miners, government teachers, road transport and electricity board staff stop work. [1][8]
8. Feb 2014: Parliament passes the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Bill (Lok Sabha 18 Feb by voice vote, Rajya Sabha 20 Feb), supported by Congress, BJP and other parties. 2 June 2014: Telangana becomes India's 29th state. Hyderabad stays the shared capital for up to 10 years. [1][9]
9. Population about 35 million (2011 census). [10]
10. Bathukamma: Telangana's autumn flower festival. Women stack flowers (tangedu, gunugu and others) in rings into a cone-shaped tower. [11]

Sources: [1] Wikipedia, "Telangana movement". [2] Report of the States Reorganisation Commission (1955), paras 369-389. [3] Wikipedia, "Gentlemen's Agreement of 1956". [4] Wikipedia, "1969 Telangana Agitation"; Telangana Today, "Students and Telangana agitation". [5] Wikipedia, "Kothapalli Jayashankar". [6] PJTSAU (pjtsau.edu.in), "About". [7] Wikipedia, "Telangana movement" (statements of 9 and 23 Dec 2009). [8] Wikipedia, "Sakala Janula Samme". [9] Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014 (India Code). [10] Census of India 2011. ## Beat formula adapted for an inspirational short
The bridge formula is deadpan comedy. Here the same skeleton carries a rise:
hook (the huge number) → setting (1956, the promise) → escalation (1969, the quiet decades, 2009, 2011) → the snap becomes the breakthrough (2014) → the explanation becomes the Bathukamma analogy → the consequence → a callback to the professor.
There is no "Luckily / Unluckily" joke line: the topic is people's sacrifice, so the voice stays warm and never jokes.

## Script v2 (English, with visual notes in brackets)
Changes from v1, made to keep every line true: line 1 no longer claims all 35 million people "waited"; "schools" became "courts" (teachers struck, but the sources list courts, not schools); the Million March "came singing" and "a million" are gone (about 50,000 came); the professor "carried it village to village" became what the sources show; 2009 credits the student protests too; "planted the idea" became "kept the idea alive" (he did not start it alone).

1. It took fifty-eight years and three generations for Telangana to find its own place on India's map.
   [hook: a map card in a crowded square, one region glowing then fading, again and again]
2. In 1956, the Telangana region of Hyderabad State was joined with Andhra to make one big Telugu state.
   [setting: golden aerial over a painted Deccan town, two halves of a map card slide together]
3. Its leaders signed a promise: Telangana's jobs, education and money would stay protected.
   [MS: a paper being signed and folded into a pocket]
4. By 1969, students said that promise was being broken, and "Jai Telangana" filled the streets.
   [glide down a street of students, crowd mode march]
5. Hundreds lost their lives. The streets went quiet, but the idea didn't.
   [golden: empty street, one lantern stays lit in a window; soft fade, no violence]
6. A professor named Jayashankar kept explaining it for nearly sixty years, in classrooms, in books, at meetings big and small.
   [day: an old teacher card with glasses and a shawl, a chalkboard, listeners of every age]
7. In December 2009, after a hunger strike by K. Chandrashekar Rao and huge student protests, Delhi said yes. Two weeks later, it said wait.
   [a far-off domed building card; a nod, then a raised palm]
8. So in 2011, Telangana simply stopped.
   [hard cut to silence: a bus halts, an office door shuts]
9. For forty-two days, buses, offices, courts, even the coal mines went still.
   [creeping inserts: idle bus, empty desk, a miner's lamp switching off]
10. A Million March was called at Tank Bund, by the lake in Hyderabad, and tens of thousands came.
   [wide over the lake, a big crowd, flower garlands]
11. In February 2014, Parliament passed the bill, with votes from rival parties.
   [the breakthrough: a gavel lands, kick shake]
12. And on the second of June, Telangana became India's twenty-ninth state.
   [white flash, fireworks over the town, day turns to golden]
13. Every year at Bathukamma, women here stack flowers into a tower, one ring at a time.
   [analogy: women stacking yellow and pink rings on a plate, close-up with lens blur]
14. No single flower makes the tower. Each ring rests on the one below.
   [the cone rises ring by ring]
15. That's how this state was built: every generation added one ring.
   [each ring dissolves into the crowd of its era: 1969 students, the professor, 2011 strikers]
16. Professor Jayashankar died in 2011, three years too early to see it.
   [the teacher's chalkboard, an empty chair]
17. So the new state named its farm university after him, the man who kept the idea alive.
   [callback: green fields at golden hour, the last flower goes on top of the Bathukamma]

Word count: 247 (about 67 s at 3.7 words/s).

## Visual rules for this topic
- No party flags or party colours (pink, green-white) anywhere. Use the national tricolour, Bathukamma flowers and the Charminar silhouette as the place marker.
- No real person's face drawn as a likeness. The professor and the strike leader are generic cards (glasses and shawl for the professor).
- No violence on screen for 1969: the loss is told through the empty street and the lantern.
- Do not show self-harm or suicide in any form.
- The 1969 death toll is never given as a number on screen or in the voice.
- Andhra is never shown as a villain. The story is about a promise and persistence, not about blame.
